WHAT I DID AS A MANAGER

How I ledthe team

Hover to exploreTap to explore+

Bigger team, same DNA

From two junior designers and one asset type to a team of eight, a manager and ten asset types. We scaled fast, but every designer worked from the same design philosophy. I was there throughout.

01 / 06

Specialists, shared

We hired an illustrator, a UX writer and a motion designer, then shared them across the product so every team got a little of the magic.

02 / 06

Built for designers, not borrowed

A company-wide playbook doesn’t fit a design team. Working with HR, we tailored ours, from titles and levels to how we assessed talent. In hiring, we looked past polished portfolios for people who made good calls quickly and felt at home in a fast-moving team.

03 / 06

Leaders, not just a leader

As the team grew, I built a second layer of managers. Designers got more one-on-one attention, decisions happened closer to the work, and I got room to focus on strategy.

04 / 06

Stretched without snapping

Contractors and an external agency kept us moving when demand surged or the team was short. The roadmap didn’t slow, and neither did the team.

05 / 06

A rhythm for collaboration

I built team cadences like weekly show and tell and peer reviews. They kept our designs consistent, let everyone build on each other’s learnings, and kept the work honest.

06 / 06

How I ledthe product

Finance, minus the jargon

Our one rule: if it needed a finance degree, it wasn’t done. Every designer lived by it, and every new hire learned it from day one. It shaped every component, product and screen.

Championing users, everywhere

Our designers made sure the user had a voice in every room, with PMs, marketing, engineering and leadership alike.

02 / 03

Speed is a design skill

Some products launched in two weeks, others in two months. The trick wasn’t cutting corners, it was working smart: reusing components, reworking pages, A/B testing and shipping small. We hired people who thrived at that pace.
