Insurance that's easy to choose and quick to buy

Upstox launched motor, life and health insurance, but while many people entered the funnel, few bought. We’d designed the flows to educate users, and an audit showed the cost: not enough people reached the quotes page. Our hypothesis: more people at quotes means more people buy. This is how we designed for speed without compromising on the right decision.

Tenet 01

Illusion of speed

Making the flow feel faster than it is

Speed isn't the number of steps. It's the number of times someone has to stop and think. We found every screen that made users pause without asking for a decision, then let it pass on its own or folded it into the screen before.

Before: After entering their car number, users landed on a "Confirm details" screen and had to tap Continue. Almost everyone tapped straight through.

After: The car appears with its model, registration and previous insurer, and the flow moves on by itself. Users still see we found the right car, which builds trust, but they don't have to approve it. One less tap, and a checkpoint became a moment of progress.

Before · Confirm, then tap Continue

After · Shown, then moves on

Tenet 02

Choose for them, then explain

Start with the answer, not the question

Some insurance decisions are hard, even for repeat buyers. So we made the call based on what we knew, showed why, and let users change it. A hard choice became a simple confirmation.

Before: Four plan types (third party, comprehensive, own damage, bumper to bumper) with no guidance. Most users couldn't tell them apart, so they guessed or left.

After: A recommended plan based on the car's age and previous policy, with a one-line reason. The other options stay one tap away, in plain language.

Before · Four options, no guidance

After · Recommended, with the reason

Tenet 03

Fetch, don't ask

If we could find it, we didn't ask for it

Every Upstox user has completed KYC, so we knew a lot before they opened insurance. Every question was a last resort: if we could find the answer, we fetched it.

Before: The first screen asked for the vehicle number, and 83% of people left there. Most didn't know it by heart and had to find their papers.

After: An integration fetches the car's registration details from the phone number we already had. Most users now open motor insurance and find their car waiting. Typing the number is the fallback, not the start.

Rinse and repeat

The team applied the same three tenets to health and life, with one designer owning each flow. Health showed quotes three steps sooner and lifted policies bought by 4.5%. Life moved only 1.2%, because most of its questions still came after the quote. The lesson was that the lift tracked how soon people saw a price, which is now where we start on any conversion problem.
